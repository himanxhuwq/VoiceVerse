The Lantern Keeper

The wind came off the sea long before the thunder did. Mira climbed the last stair of the lighthouse, the lantern swinging in her hand, and set it down beside the great dark lens.

“Keep it lit, whatever comes,” old Tomas had told her that morning, coughing into his sleeve. “Ships are counting on it. So am I.”

By dusk the sky had turned the colour of a bruise. Rain hammered the glass. Below, the waves rose black and white against the rocks, and the door at the foot of the tower banged open, then shut, then open again.

Then, somewhere inside the storm, a voice that was not the wind began to sing.
